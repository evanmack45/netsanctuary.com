Can someone else send my Texas privacy request for me? What the law says about authorized agents

Short answer

Yes, for opt-outs. Section 541.055(e) lets you name an authorized agent to opt out of targeted advertising and the sale of personal data, and a company must comply if it can verify you and the agent's authority. The chapter uses the term authorized agent only there. Whitepages' notice says an agent can send a delete request with signed written permission.

Checked against the sources below on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, America/Chicago. This is a reading guide, not legal advice, and we are not your agent.

What the statute says

A consumer may designate another person to serve as the consumer's authorized agent and act on the consumer's behalf to opt out of the processing of the consumer's personal data under Sections 541.051(b)(5)(A) and (B).

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 541 (Texas Data Privacy and Security Act), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

Those two items are targeted advertising and the sale of personal data. Chapter 541 uses the term authorized agent only in section 541.055(e) and the lines that follow it. For a child, section 541.051(a) has a separate rule, covered on the child's data page.

A company can refuse an agent's request in some cases. Two of them:

the authorized agent does not communicate the request to the controller in a clear and unambiguous manner;

the controller is not able to verify, with commercially reasonable effort, that the consumer is a resident of this state;

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 541 (Texas Data Privacy and Security Act), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

What one company says about agents

Whitepages' notice has an Authorized Agents paragraph in the section that covers Texas residents:

You may submit a request to know or a request to delete your Personal Information through an authorized agent. If you do so, the agent must present signed written permission to act on your behalf and you may also be required to independently verify your identity with us.

From Consumer Privacy Rights, Whitepages.

That is one company's rule. The statute does not set a signed-permission requirement, and other companies can set their own.

What these pages do not say

  • That an agent can send a deletion request under the statute. Section 541.055(e) covers opt-outs.
  • What proof of authority a company will accept. The statute says only that the company must be able to verify it.
