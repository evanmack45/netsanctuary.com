Can I sue a company that ignored my Texas privacy request?

Short answer

Not under this law. Section 541.156 says the chapter cannot be construed as providing a basis for a private right of action, and section 541.151 gives the Attorney General exclusive authority to enforce it. Before suing, the Attorney General must give written notice and 30 days to cure. A civil penalty of up to $7,500 per violation applies only after that.

Checked against the sources below on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, America/Chicago. This is a reading guide, not legal advice, and we are not your agent.

What the statute says

Section 541.156 is titled No private right of action. Its full text:

This chapter may not be construed as providing a basis for, or being subject to, a private right of action for a violation of this chapter or any other law.

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 541 (Texas Data Privacy and Security Act), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

Section 541.151 says who enforces the chapter instead:

The attorney general has exclusive authority to enforce this chapter.

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 541 (Texas Data Privacy and Security Act), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

The Attorney General's own page on the law agrees:

The Act does not provide a private right of action.

From Texas Data Privacy and Security Act, Texas Attorney General.

The sentence in 541.156 ends with "or any other law". What that does to a claim under a different statute is a legal question this page does not answer.

What the Attorney General has to do first

If the attorney general has reasonable cause to believe that a person has engaged in or is engaging in a violation of this chapter, the attorney general may issue a civil investigative demand.

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 541 (Texas Data Privacy and Security Act), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

That is section 541.153(a). Section 541.154 sets a notice and cure step before any lawsuit:

Before bringing an action under Section 541.155, the attorney general shall notify a person in writing, not later than the 30th day before bringing the action, identifying the specific provisions of this chapter the attorney general alleges have been or are being violated.

The attorney general may not bring an action against the person if:

within the 30-day period, the person cures the identified violation; and

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 541 (Texas Data Privacy and Security Act), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

To rely on a cure, the company must also give the Attorney General a written statement. Among the items listed in 541.154(2):

notified the consumer that the consumer's privacy violation was addressed, if the consumer's contact information has been made available to the person;

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 541 (Texas Data Privacy and Security Act), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

Penalties, and where the money goes

Section 541.155 covers a company that does not cure:

A person who violates this chapter following the cure period described by Section 541.154 or who breaches a written statement provided to the attorney general under that section is liable for a civil penalty in an amount not to exceed $7,500 for each violation.

The attorney general shall deposit a civil penalty collected under this section in accordance with Section 402.007, Government Code.

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 541 (Texas Data Privacy and Security Act), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

The Attorney General's page lists what an enforcement action can seek:

The Texas Attorney General has exclusive authority to enforce the Act, and may issue civil investigative demands, and file enforcement actions to obtain civil penalties, injunctive relief, attorney's fees, and costs.

From Texas Data Privacy and Security Act, Texas Attorney General.

Section 541.155 does not provide for a payment to the consumer.

What you can do

Section 541.152(2) requires the Attorney General to post an online complaint mechanism. When to complain to the Attorney General covers what the complaint page says. The Attorney General's page also says:

Texas law prohibits the Office of the Attorney General from providing legal advice, opinions, or representation to private individuals.

From Texas Data Privacy and Security Act, Texas Attorney General.

If a company has ignored a deletion request, the 45-day page has the response clock.

What these pages do not say

  • Any payment to the person who was harmed. Section 541.155 sends civil penalties to the state.
  • That the Attorney General will act on one person's complaint.
  • Whether a claim under another law, such as a general consumer protection statute, is open to you. We did not research that. Ask a lawyer.
