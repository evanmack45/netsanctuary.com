Can a company charge more or refuse service if I use my rights under the Texas privacy law?

Short answer

Generally no. Section 541.101(b)(3) bars discrimination for using any of the consumer rights, including denying goods or services, charging more, or providing a different level of quality. Subsection (c) carves out a company's duty to provide a service that needs data it does not hold, a different charge after an opt-out, and bona fide loyalty or club card programs.

Fees for answering a request are separate

A fee for handling your request falls under section 541.052(d), not this rule. The fee page covers it. For opting out of sales and targeted ads, see opt out of sale.

If a company does it anyway

The Attorney General enforces the Act, and the statute gives no private right of action. Can I sue covers that, and the complaint page covers the Attorney General's online complaint form.
