Can a Texas parent ask data brokers to delete a child's data?

Short answer

Yes, for a known child under 13. Section 541.051(a) lets a parent or legal guardian exercise the child's rights. The statute does not give the same wording for teenagers. The letter builder in this pack is written for an adult asking about their own data and has no parent version.

Checked against the sources below on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, America/Chicago. This is a reading guide, not legal advice, and we are not your agent.

What the statute says

With respect to the processing of personal data belonging to a known child, a parent or legal guardian of the child may exercise the consumer rights on behalf of the child.

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 541 (Texas Data Privacy and Security Act), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

The definitions that matter:

"Child" means an individual younger than 13 years of age.

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 541 (Texas Data Privacy and Security Act), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

Proof

A company may ask you to prove you are the parent or guardian. Section 541.052(e) lets it hold a request until it can confirm it:

If a controller is unable to authenticate the request using commercially reasonable efforts, the controller is not required to comply with a consumer request submitted under Section 541.051

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 541 (Texas Data Privacy and Security Act), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

Brokers that hold a child's data

A registered broker that knows it holds a child's data must file extra statements. Section 510.005(b)(5) lists them:

a statement detailing the data collection practices, databases, sales activities, and opt-out policies that are applicable to the personal data of a known child; and

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 510 (Data Brokers), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

That statement is part of the broker's registry entry, so the registry is one place to look for it.

What this pack does for a parent

The letter builder says "I am sending this request myself. No one else is acting for me." That is wrong for a parent. A parent would need to edit the letter by hand and say they act for the child. We have not built a parent version.

DROP Texas says its form has a parent path:

A parent or legal guardian can choose My child on the request form for a child under 13 and send the requests for free.

From Children's data, DROP Texas.

That is DROP Texas's own description. We have not tested its form. The one-form page compares it with this pack.

What these pages do not say

  • A rule for teenagers. The statute text quoted here covers a known child under 13, and we did not find matching wording for ages 13 to 17.
  • What a company will accept as proof of guardianship.
