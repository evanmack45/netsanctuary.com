Can I make a company correct inaccurate personal data about me under the Texas privacy law?

Short answer

Yes, for a covered company. Section 541.051(b)(2) gives you the right to correct inaccuracies in your personal data, taking into account the nature of the personal data and the purposes of the processing. The statute does not say what proof you must give or exactly what the company must change.

What the statute says

Section 541.051(b) lists the rights. The second is:

The Attorney General's page on the Act has the same right in plain words:

Read it in: Texas Data Privacy and Security Act.

The closing clause gives the company room. It sets no test for what counts as an inaccuracy, and it sets no standard of proof for you.

Correction is not deletion

Deleting data is a separate right in 541.051(b)(3):

delete personal data provided by or obtained about the consumer;

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 541 (Texas Data Privacy and Security Act), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

If the aim is to remove a record, not fix it, that is the right to use. If a data broker's listing has your details wrong, a correction request and a removal request are different asks. Remove my home address covers removal.

Where the right stops

The right reaches personal data only. The definition says:

Read it in: Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 541 (Texas Data Privacy and Security Act).

So a company can argue that a listing copied from public records is outside the right. People-search said no covers that refusal. The Act also covers only companies it names, and only Texas residents acting in an individual or household context. See small business coverage.
