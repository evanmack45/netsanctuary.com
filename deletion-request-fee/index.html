Can a company charge me a fee to act on my request under the Texas privacy law?

Short answer

Not for an ordinary request. Section 541.052(d) says a controller shall provide information in response to a consumer request free of charge, at least twice annually per consumer. It can charge a reasonable fee, or decline, only if a request is manifestly unfounded, excessive, or repetitive, and it bears the burden of showing that.

What section 541.052(d) says

A controller shall provide information in response to a consumer request free of charge, at least twice annually per consumer.

If a request from a consumer is manifestly unfounded, excessive, or repetitive, the controller may charge the consumer a reasonable fee to cover the administrative costs of complying with the request or may decline to act on the request.

The controller bears the burden of demonstrating for purposes of this subsection that a request is manifestly unfounded, excessive, or repetitive.

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 541 (Texas Data Privacy and Security Act), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

Subsection (d) says "a consumer request" and does not single out deletion. It sits in the section that governs responses to every rights request. Subsection (a) says:

a controller shall comply with a request submitted by a consumer to exercise the consumer's rights pursuant to Section 541.051 as provided by this section.

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 541 (Texas Data Privacy and Security Act), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

The Attorney General's summary differs in one word

The Attorney General's page on the law says:

A company's response to a consumer request must be free of charge, up to twice annually per consumer

unless the request is unfounded, excessive, or repetitive, in which case the consumer may be charged a reasonable administrative cost.

From Texas Data Privacy and Security Act, Texas Attorney General.

The statute says "at least twice annually". The page says "up to twice annually". The statute is the law. We have not found an Attorney General explanation of the difference.

What the statute leaves open

The chapter does not define "manifestly unfounded, excessive, or repetitive", and it sets no dollar figure for a reasonable fee. It limits the fee to the company's administrative costs of complying. Because the company has the burden, a fee demand without an explanation is something to ask about in writing.

A company that charges a fee or declines on this ground is declining to act on your request in part. Section 541.052(c) then requires it to say why and how to appeal. Appeal a denied request covers that step.

Other limits that touch cost and effort

A controller may not require a consumer to create a new account to exercise the consumer's rights under this subchapter but may require a consumer to use an existing account.

Any provision of a contract or agreement that waives or limits in any way a consumer right described by Sections 541.051, 541.052, and 541.053 is contrary to public policy and is void and unenforceable.

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 541 (Texas Data Privacy and Security Act), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

Those are sections 541.055(b) and 541.054. For the 45-day response clock, see the 45-day page. Enforcement is by the Attorney General only: the complaint page.

What these pages do not say

  • A definition of "manifestly unfounded," "excessive," or "repetitive." Section 541.001 defines none of them.
  • A dollar limit on a reasonable fee. The statute caps it only by the administrative cost of complying.
  • That a company must erase data for free in every case. Subsection (d) covers responding to a request, and section 541.052(e) and (f) set other conditions.
