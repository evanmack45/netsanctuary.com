Do I have to live in Texas to use its privacy law?

Short answer

On the statute's text, yes. Section 541.001(7) defines a consumer as an individual who is a resident of this state acting only in an individual or household context. It excludes an individual acting in a commercial or employment context. The statute does not say how a company checks residency or what happens if you move.

The definition

Section 541.001(7) defines who the law's rights belong to:

"Consumer" means an individual who is a resident of this state acting only in an individual or household context.

The term does not include an individual acting in a commercial or employment context.

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 541 (Texas Data Privacy and Security Act), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

The rights in section 541.051 are given to that consumer:

A consumer is entitled to exercise the consumer rights authorized by this section at any time by submitting a request to a controller specifying the consumer rights the consumer wishes to exercise.

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 541 (Texas Data Privacy and Security Act), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

Read together, a person who is not a resident of Texas is not a "consumer" under this chapter. Other states have their own laws. This page covers only Texas.

Individual context, not business or work

Data about you as a private person fits the definition. Data about you acting in a commercial or employment context does not. The statute does not say how to treat a record that mixes your personal and business details, such as a listing under your own name for a business you run.

The company has its own tests

Being a Texas resident is half of it. Section 541.002(a) says the chapter applies only to a person that:

conducts business in this state or produces a product or service consumed by residents of this state;

processes or engages in the sale of personal data; and

is not a small business as defined by the United States Small Business Administration, except to the extent that Section 541.107 applies to a person described by this subdivision.

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 541 (Texas Data Privacy and Security Act), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

The Attorney General's page describes the same reach: it says the safeguards apply to companies that "conduct business in [Texas] or produce a product or service consumed by residents of [Texas]". The small-business page covers the third test. Section 541.002(b) also lists entities the chapter does not apply to, including nonprofit organizations and financial institutions.

What the statute does not say

The text has no residency proof requirement and no rule for a move. What it says about proving who you are is about identity, not address. Section 541.052(e) lets a company that cannot authenticate a request decline it and ask for more information. The 45-day page has that text.

What these pages do not say

  • How a company confirms you live in Texas. The statute names no residency proof.
  • What happens to a request if you move out of Texas after sending it. The statute does not address a move.
  • Whether a non-resident can use another state's law against a company covered here. This page covers Texas only.
