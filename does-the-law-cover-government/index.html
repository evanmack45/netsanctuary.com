Does the Texas privacy law cover a city or police cameras?

Short answer

Not the government body itself. Section 541.002(b)(1) says the chapter does not apply to a state agency or a political subdivision of this state. The exemption names the government body. It does not name a company that sells cameras or software to one, and the chapter has no text on police or cameras.

Checked against the sources below, 25 Nov 2026. Not legal advice.

What the Act says

Section 541.002(b) of the Business & Commerce Code lists who the chapter does not apply to. The first item:

Read it in: Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 541 (Texas Data Privacy and Security Act).

The Attorney General's summary page says the same:

Read it in: Texas Data Privacy and Security Act.

"State agency" is defined, in section 541.001(30):

Read it in: Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 541 (Texas Data Privacy and Security Act).

"Political subdivision" has no definition in chapter 541. A city or a county is the everyday example, but this page cannot point to statute text in the chapter that says so.

What it means for a city or a police department

On the text and ordinary usage, a city or county is a political subdivision, and a police department is part of one. If so, the chapter does not apply to it, and an access, correction, deletion or opt-out request under the Act sent to it is outside the statute. The Act also speaks to local rules:

Read it in: Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 541 (Texas Data Privacy and Security Act).

That is section 541.205. It is about ordinances on processing by controllers and processors. We have not found a source on how it applies to a city's own cameras.
