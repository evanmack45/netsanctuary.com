Is there one Texas opt-out form for data brokers? What DROP Texas does and what this pack does

Short answer

DROP Texas is one free form, run by a private company, that sends opt-out-of-sale requests to the companies it names, nine of them on its home page. This pack sends nothing. It writes deletion letters you send yourself, to any company. For the companies DROP Texas names, it is less work. This pack has a reason to exist only in the cases below.

Checked against the sources below on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, America/Chicago. This is a reading guide, not legal advice, and we are not your agent.

What DROP Texas says it is

DROP Texas is a free service with a form you use to ask data brokers not to sell your personal information. You fill out one form and send the requests. Brokers decide whether they have a record and how they respond.

DROP Texas is an independent, free service run by a private enterprise. It is not a government website and is not affiliated with the State of Texas or the Texas Attorney General.

From Privacy help for Texans who use the DROP Texas form, DROP Texas.

The home page carousel names DMAchoice, Catalog Choice, Acxiom, Epsilon, Equifax, Experian, TransUnion, Spokeo, Whitepages.

From Data brokers: what they hold and how Texans opt out, DROP Texas.

Its own pages say plainly that the form is not a deletion tool:

This form does not delete personal information a broker already holds. It is an opt-out of sale you send yourself.

DROP Texas does not check that a broker deleted a record.

From Delete personal information: a Texas opt-out guide, DROP Texas.

What it asks you for

The form asks for your name (including an optional middle name), date of birth, mailing address, email, Texas residency, and, if you choose to provide it, your phone number.

Your information is stored securely and auto-deleted per our retention policy

What this pack does

  • It runs in your browser. It sends nothing and stores nothing. See what we store.
  • Its letter needs your name and Texas city. The email and listing address are optional. It does not ask for a birth date or a mailing address.
  • It can ask a company to delete your data and to stop selling it, in one letter, or to give you access to it.
  • It has an appeal letter and a tracker with a reply-by date 45 days after you send.
  • It works for any company, including companies DROP Texas does not name.

A limit on the deletion difference

A deletion letter does not guarantee erasure. For data a company got from another source, section 541.052(f) says the company is treated as compliant with a deletion request if it

retaining a record of the deletion request and the minimum data necessary for the purpose of ensuring the consumer's personal data remains deleted from the business's records and not using the retained data for any other purpose under this chapter; or

opting the consumer out of the processing of that personal data for any purpose other than a purpose that is exempt under the provisions of this chapter.

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 541 (Texas Data Privacy and Security Act), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

So a broker can answer a deletion letter by suppressing your record, which is close to what an opt-out does. The difference is real in the words of the request. It can be small in the result.

Verdict

Use DROP Texas if you want one form for the companies it names and you are willing to give it a name, birth date, mailing address, and email. It is less work than this pack, and we cannot beat that.

Use this pack if one of these is true:

  • You want to ask for deletion or access, not only an opt-out of sale.
  • You need to contact a company DROP Texas does not name.
  • You do not want to give a third party your birth date and mailing address.
  • You want an appeal letter and a deadline tracker for each company.

If none of those fits you, DROP Texas is the better tool and you do not need this pack. Neither tool is run by the state, and neither can make a company act. The pack is new. We have not sent its letters to brokers and we cannot say how any company will answer.
