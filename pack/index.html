The free Texas data broker pack

Five steps. You do all of them. We see none of it.

Not legal advice. We are not your agent. We do not send, file, or follow up on anything for you, and we never ask for your ID.

Step 1. Make a short list of companies

Start with the companies you already know hold your information. Then use these three routes.

The Texas registry

Search the Texas Secretary of State's Data Broker Registry by company name. By law, each entry includes a link to the company's own page that explains how to use your Texas privacy rights. Use that page first. Some companies have a form, and some give an email address. Source: Bus. & Com. Code section 510.005(b)(2-a).

The registry lists only brokers that meet the law's size tests, and it does not delete anything. Sources: section 510.003; Texas Secretary of State.

People-search sites with their own opt-out page

  • Spokeo opt-out page. Spokeo emails you a confirmation link, and you click it yourself.
  • Whitepages opt-out page. Its first step asks you to paste the address of your profile page.

Opting out on a site does not remove the record from the public source it came from.

Step 2. Write the letter

Nothing you type here leaves this page. The page makes no network requests and saves nothing. Close the tab and the text is gone, so copy or download what you want to keep.

Which letter?
What you are asking for

The builder never asks for an ID number, a birth date, or a street address, and you should not type one.

Open in my email app

"Open in my email app" starts a draft in your own mail program with the letter in it. You add the company's address and press send. If the draft comes up empty, use Copy letter.

No JavaScript, or you want plain files? Download the templates.
  • Request: delete my data and stop selling it (.txt)
  • Request: tell me what you have (.txt)
  • Appeal (.txt)

Fill in the square brackets in a text editor.

Step 3. Send it yourself

Use the company's own Texas privacy page from step 1. If it has a form, paste the letter in. If it gives an email address, send the letter from your own email. Keep a copy.

A company may ask you to confirm it is you. You decide what to send. Source: Bus. & Com. Code section 541.052(e).

Step 4. Track the date

The company has up to 45 days after it receives your request. It can extend once by 45 more days if it tells you why inside the first 45. Source: section 541.052(b).

Download the tracker (.csv)

It opens in any spreadsheet program. The reply-by dates in it are for you to fill in. The builder above shows the first date to check.

Step 5. If the answer is no, or silence

  • If the company says no, it has to tell you why and how to appeal. Use the appeal letter above. It has to answer your appeal in writing within 60 days. Source: sections 541.052(c) and 541.053.
  • If it denies the appeal, it has to tell you how to complain to the Texas Attorney General. You can file a privacy complaint online at the Attorney General's privacy page. Source: sections 541.053(d) and 541.152.
  • Some data will stay. Both Texas laws leave out publicly available information, and small companies are outside the Act. Check your listings again later, because records can reappear. Sources: sections 541.001(19), 541.001(27), 541.002(a)(3); Spokeo.
