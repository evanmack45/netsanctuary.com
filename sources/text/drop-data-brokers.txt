Data brokers: what they hold and how Texans opt out Data brokers Data brokers collect, process, or transfer personal data they did not collect from you in person. They can then sell a profile, or access to one, to other businesses. You often have no account there, so you never chose the sale. What an opt-out asks data brokers An opt-out request asks a data broker not to sell your personal information. It is not a promise that the broker deletes every copy, and it does not pull back data already sold. DROP Texas does not check that a broker deleted a record. Companies this site names The home page carousel names DMAchoice, Catalog Choice, Acxiom, Epsilon, Equifax, Experian, TransUnion, Spokeo, Whitepages. Each name has a card on the brokers page, with categories taken from that company’s own privacy notice. Names are not added to that list until they are in the broker registry this project uses. Some of those companies also act as consumer reporting agencies. An opt-out of sale is not a credit freeze and is not a dispute of a credit file. A freeze is a separate step you take with the bureaus. The FTC explains it at consumer.ftc.gov. Texas data broker registration Texas also has a data broker registration law. It was Chapter 509 of the Business and Commerce Code and was redesignated as Chapter 510, effective September 1, 2025.
