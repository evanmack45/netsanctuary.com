Delete personal information: a Texas opt-out guide Delete personal information This form does not delete personal information a broker already holds. It is an opt-out of sale you send yourself. DROP Texas gives you one free form for that request. Brokers decide whether they have a record and how they respond. Who can delete personal information here Anyone can use the form. A checked Texas box records the Texas Data Privacy and Security Act. An unchecked box still uses that law for now and is marked non-Texas. A parent or guardian can send a request for a child under 13. Teens 13 to 17 can submit for themselves. Read protecting children's information for that path. What an opt-out request is An opt-out of sale asks a company not to sell personal information it holds about you. It is not the same as a deletion request, a credit freeze, or a listing on the National Do Not Call Registry. Those are separate steps. You use this form to send opt-out requests. It does not freeze credit or enroll you in Do Not Call. What DROP Texas does You say whether you live in Texas, enter the details the request uses, and submit. Your requests go to the companies named on the brokers page. There is no fee. The how it works page walks through the form. What this does not do DROP Texas does not check that a broker deleted a record. It does not pull back copies already sold. It does not place a credit freeze. If a status is shown later, it is one of Submitted, Confirmed, Pending, Not completed, or No record found. None of those words means a deletion was verified.
