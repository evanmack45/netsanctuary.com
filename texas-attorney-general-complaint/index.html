When can I complain to the Texas Attorney General about a data request?

Short answer

The Attorney General alone enforces the Texas privacy law and runs an online complaint form. The law tells a company to point you there after it denies your appeal. The AG's page says complaints are public, warns against including your birth date or ID, and says a complaint number does not mean a case is open.

Checked against the sources below on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, America/Chicago. This is a reading guide, not legal advice, and we are not your agent.

What the statute says

If the controller denies an appeal, the controller shall provide the consumer with the online mechanism described by Section 541.152 through which the consumer may contact the attorney general to submit a complaint.

The attorney general has exclusive authority to enforce this chapter.

an online mechanism through which a consumer may submit a complaint under this chapter to the attorney general.

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 541 (Texas Data Privacy and Security Act), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

Section 541.152(2) is the source of that last line. The statute does not say a complaint must wait until an appeal is denied.

What the Attorney General's page says

The Privacy complaint form is used for complaints that your personal information is being unlawfully collected, shared, or mishandled by a business.

Know that under Texas law your complaint is open to the public.

Regardless of the form you select, DO NOT INCLUDE sensitive or confidential information in your complaint such as your date of birth, Social Security Number, government-issued ID, or financial account numbers.

From File a Consumer Complaint, Texas Attorney General.

Have the business name and its full address ready. The page says the form cannot save a half-finished complaint:

Be ready to complete the complaint form in full because the system cannot save your in-progress complaint.

From File a Consumer Complaint, Texas Attorney General.

What to expect after you file

The number assigned to your complaint is strictly for reference. It does not indicate that any decision has been made regarding the merits of the complaint and does not reflect that the OAG has a case or investigation pending.

Your complaint will be reviewed for informational purposes, helping the Attorney General monitor consumer protection issues throughout the State of Texas.

From File a Consumer Complaint, Texas Attorney General.

Texas law prohibits the Office of the Attorney General from providing legal advice, opinions, or representation to private individuals.

From Texas Data Privacy and Security Act, Texas Attorney General.

What these pages do not say

  • That the Attorney General will act on one person's complaint. The page says complaints are reviewed for information.
  • A deadline for filing or for a reply.
