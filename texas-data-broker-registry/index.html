Is there a Texas data broker registry, and does it remove me from anything?

Short answer

Yes, there is a registry, and no, it does not remove you from anything. The Secretary of State keeps it as a list. By law each entry includes a contact for the broker and a link to the broker's page on how to use your Texas privacy rights, so it is a place to find where to send a request.

Checked against the sources below on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, America/Chicago. This is a reading guide, not legal advice, and we are not your agent.

What the registry is

Chapter 510 of the Business and Commerce Code is the Texas data broker law. It says:

The secretary of state shall establish and maintain, on its Internet website, a searchable, central registry of data brokers registered under Section 510.005.

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 510 (Data Brokers), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

The Secretary of State's own page describes the same registry:

The Office of the Secretary of State maintains a searchable, central Data Broker Registry of registered data brokers as required by Business and Commerce Code Section 510.006.

From Data Brokers, Texas Secretary of State.

What each entry holds

Section 510.005(b) lists what a broker must file. Two items matter for a request. The first is a contact:

a contact person and the primary physical address, e-mail address, telephone number, and Internet website address for the data broker;

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 510 (Data Brokers), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

The second is a link to the broker's own instructions:

a link to a page on the data broker's Internet website that provides consumers with specific instructions, which must be prominently displayed, on how to exercise their consumer rights under Section 541.051, and any other applicable data privacy rights under Chapter 541;

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 510 (Data Brokers), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

The registry must show that filing:

for each data broker, the information filed under Section 510.005(b).

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 510 (Data Brokers), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

What the registry does not do

The Secretary of State says it is only the filing office:

The Secretary of State is the filing officer for data broker registration and does not have the authority to regulate the business practices of a data broker, investigate alleged violations by the data broker, or enforce the requirements referenced above.

From Data Brokers, Texas Secretary of State.

Its FAQ sends complaints to a different office:

A person that believes a data broker is not registered as required or has otherwise committed a violation may file a complaint with the Attorney General's Consumer Protection Division or contact that agency directly for additional information.

From Frequently Asked Questions for Data Brokers, Texas Secretary of State.

Not every company that sells data has to register. The law says:

To conduct business in this state, a data broker to which this chapter applies shall register with the secretary of state by filing a registration statement and paying a registration fee of $300.

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 510 (Data Brokers), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

How to use it

Search the Secretary of State's registry for a company name. Open its entry, find the rights page link, and send your request there or to the listed contact. The free pack has a letter builder and a tracker for that. We did not read the registry's entries for this page, because the registry runs as a browser app.

What these pages do not say

  • A way to ask for deletion through the registry. The Secretary of State pages never mention deletion.
  • Which companies are in the registry today. We did not search it.
  • Whether a company that is not listed has to answer you. Chapter 541 has its own size tests.
