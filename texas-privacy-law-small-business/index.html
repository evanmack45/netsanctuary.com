Does the Texas privacy law apply to small businesses, and who is exempt?

Short answer

Mostly no. The Act applies to a company that does business in Texas, processes or sells personal data, and is not a small business by the U.S. Small Business Administration's definition. One exception: a small business needs your prior consent before it sells sensitive data. Nonprofits, colleges, state agencies, and some financial and health entities are also outside it.

Checked against the sources below on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, America/Chicago. This is a reading guide, not legal advice, and we are not your agent.

Who the Act covers

Section 541.002(a) sets the test. It says the chapter

applies only to a person that:

conducts business in this state or produces a product or service consumed by residents of this state;

processes or engages in the sale of personal data; and

is not a small business as defined by the United States Small Business Administration, except to the extent that Section 541.107 applies to a person described by this subdivision.

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 541 (Texas Data Privacy and Security Act), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

The statute points to the SBA's definition and gives no revenue or headcount number of its own. We did not look up the SBA's size standards, and we cannot tell you whether a given company is a small business.

The sensitive-data exception

Section 541.107 puts one duty on small businesses:

A person described by Section 541.002(a)(3) may not engage in the sale of personal data that is sensitive data without receiving prior consent from the consumer.

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 541 (Texas Data Privacy and Security Act), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

What counts as sensitive data is on the sensitive data page.

Others the Act does not apply to

Section 541.002(b) lists entities outside the chapter. Among them:

a state agency or a political subdivision of this state;

a financial institution or data subject to Title V, Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (15 U.S.C. Section 6801 et seq.);

a nonprofit organization;

an institution of higher education;

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 541 (Texas Data Privacy and Security Act), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

The list also names covered entities and business associates under the federal health privacy rules (HIPAA) and some electric utilities.

Data brokers have their own size test

The data broker registration law, chapter 510, uses a different test. Section 510.003(a) says the chapter

applies only to a data broker that, in a 12-month period, derives:

more than 50 percent of the data broker's revenue directly from processing or transferring personal data not collected by the data broker directly from the individuals to whom the data pertains; or

revenue directly from processing or transferring the personal data of more than 50,000 individuals not collected by the data broker directly from the individuals to whom the data pertains.

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 510 (Data Brokers), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

A company can be too small for one law and covered by the other. The registry page explains what registration does.

What these pages do not say

  • A revenue or headcount number for a small business. The statute points to the SBA definition and gives none.
  • Whether a particular company is covered. We did not check any company's size.
  • That a company outside the Act will ignore you. Some offer their own opt-out forms anyway.
