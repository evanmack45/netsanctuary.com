What counts as sensitive data under the Texas privacy law, and when is my consent required?

Short answer

Section 541.001 lists sensitive data: data revealing race or ethnicity, religion, a health diagnosis, sexuality, or citizenship or immigration status; genetic or biometric data used to identify you; data from a known child; and precise geolocation. A company covered by the Act may not process it without your consent. A small business may not sell it without your prior consent.

The list

The definitions section says:

"Sensitive data" means a category of personal data. The term includes:

personal data revealing racial or ethnic origin, religious beliefs, mental or physical health diagnosis, sexuality, or citizenship or immigration status;

genetic or biometric data that is processed for the purpose of uniquely identifying an individual;

personal data collected from a known child; or

precise geolocation data.

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 541 (Texas Data Privacy and Security Act), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

The consent rule

Section 541.101(b) lists what a company covered by the Act may not do. One item is:

process the sensitive data of a consumer without obtaining the consumer's consent, or, in the case of processing the sensitive data of a known child, without processing that data in accordance with the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act of 1998 (15 U.S.C. Section 6501 et seq.).

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 541 (Texas Data Privacy and Security Act), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

For a known child, the rule points to the federal children's privacy law. The child's data page covers who may send a request for a child.

Small businesses and sales

A small business is outside most of the Act, with one exception. Section 541.107 says:

A person described by Section 541.002(a)(3) may not engage in the sale of personal data that is sensitive data without receiving prior consent from the consumer.

From Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 541 (Texas Data Privacy and Security Act), Texas Constitution and Statutes.

The small business page has the full applicability test.

Using this with a request

The Act lets you ask a company to confirm whether it processes your personal data and to give you access to it. The free pack letter has a box for that request. The statute does not say a company must tell you which of your data it treats as sensitive.

What these pages do not say

  • Whether a given company holds sensitive data about you. Only the company can say.
  • What a valid consent form must say. We quote only the rule that consent is required.
